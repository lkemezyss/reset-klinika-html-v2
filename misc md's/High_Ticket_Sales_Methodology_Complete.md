## 1. Core Philosophy

The entire methodology rests on a few foundational truths:

- **People make decisions based on needs, not wants.** Wanting something triggers dopamine (future-focused, feel-good). Needing something triggers cortisol (present-focused, urgency). Your job is to move the prospect from "I want" to "I need."
- **Feelings drive decisions.** You create feelings by getting people to *remember experiences.* The more recent the experience, the stronger the emotional recall.
- **You are selling identity change, not a product.** The deepest close happens when the prospect recognizes that their current identity (how they make decisions, what they believe about themselves) is the thing keeping them stuck — and they commit to becoming someone new.
- **Never buy into the prospect's frame.** If they minimize their pain, laugh it off, or give you surface-level answers, it is your job to slow them down, call it out, and get them to sit with the discomfort. That discomfort is what creates the motivation to change.
- **Leadership, not friendship.** You dictate the pace of the call. You are a doctor diagnosing a problem, not a friend agreeing with their rationalizations.

## 2. Top of the Call — Getting Outcomes

The first few minutes of the call determine everything. You need four things in a specific order before you can build pain or move into alignment.

### The Sequence

| Step | What You're Getting | Example Question |
|------|-------------------|-----------------|
| **1. Tangible Want** | A specific, drawable goal — not vague aspirations | *"What's the main thing you're looking to achieve by getting some help with [X]?"* |
| **2. Current State** | Where they are right now relative to the goal | *"Where are you at now? Like the last two weeks / last month?"* |
| **3. Specific Need** | What they believe they need to close the gap | *"What do you need specifically so you can get there?"* |
| **4. Experience / Noticing** | What they're observing that makes them feel they need that | *"What are you noticing that's causing you to feel like you need [X]?"* |

### Rules for Getting Tangible Goals

The "wheelbarrow test" — if you can't draw it on a piece of paper or put it in a wheelbarrow (conceptually), it's not tangible enough.

- ❌ "I want to take care of my family" → too vague
- ✅ "I want to retire my parents" → you can visualize this
- ❌ "I want consistency" → means different things to different people
- ✅ "I want to consistently hit 10K months with a 70/30 win rate" → specific, measurable

**How to drill for tangibles:** When they give you something vague, gently push:

- *"Better can mean different things for different people. What is it specifically you're trying to [do/provide/achieve]?"*
- *"Like, give me an idea — what does that look like for you?"*
- *"Can you put a number on that?"*

### Why the Sequence Matters

If you skip straight to pain points, the call takes longer and you lose structure. The sequence builds a logical chain:

1. Here's where I want to be → (dopamine, engagement)
2. Here's where I am now → (gap created)
3. Here's what I think I need → (self-diagnosis, ownership)
4. Here's what I'm experiencing that tells me I need it → (cortisol, feeling, urgency)

Step 4 is where you attach *remembered experience* to the *stated need.* This is the emotional foundation for the rest of the call.

### Wants vs. Needs Language

Always redirect "want" language into "need" language:

- Prospect: *"I want to leave my job."*
- You: *"I get you want to leave your job. Could I share a perspective? Which person shows up in a better mindset — the guy that wants to do something, or the guy that needs to? Is this something you want to do, or something you need to do?"*
- Prospect: *"I need to."*
- You: *"Why though?"*

This shift from want → need triggers cortisol instead of dopamine, creating urgency rather than comfortable daydreaming.

## 3. Building the Problem — Creating Urgency

Once you have the four outcomes from the top of the call, you shift into building the emotional weight of the problem.

### Cortisol vs. Dopamine — The Science

- **Dopamine** = "I want Chipotle later." Future-focused. No urgency. The prospect can picture a nice future and feel good without taking action.
- **Cortisol** = "I need food because I haven't eaten in two days." Present-focused. Creates the feeling that something must change *now.*

Your entire job in the problem-building phase is to create cortisol spikes by getting them to recall recent, emotionally charged experiences.

### Shrink the Time Gap

The closer the experience is to the present moment, the more urgency it creates. It is harder for someone to remember how they felt five months ago than how they felt yesterday.

- ❌ *"How has that been going for the last year?"* → too distant, too easy to rationalize
- ✅ *"Give me the last couple of weeks. What have you been noticing?"* → recent, vivid, emotionally accessible

If the prospect starts going far back in time, gently redirect: *"Oh, apologies — I mean more like the last two to three weeks. Give me an idea of what that's looked like recently."*

### Probe on the Feeling Word

When a prospect uses an emotional word — "annoying," "frustrating," "stressful," "depressing" — that is gold. Stop and probe on it.

- Prospect: *"It's just annoying."*
- You: *"Annoying in what way?"*
- You: *"How long have you been feeling that way?"*
- You: *"What do you see happening if you keep showing up every day in that annoyed mindset?"*

Do **not** skip past feeling words to get to the next question. The feeling word is the entry point into genuine emotional engagement.

### Don't Force a Problem That Isn't There

If the prospect says they're struggling with "revenge trading," don't ask "How has that been affecting your confidence?" if they never mentioned confidence. You are trying to force them into a problem they may not have — and this will trigger defensiveness.

Instead, stay with what they actually said: *"How has that been impacting you?"* Let them tell you the real problem in their own words.

### Building Doubt When They Minimize

When a prospect is minimizing ("I'm slowly getting there," "It's not that bad," "I'm figuring it out"), you need to pull back and build doubt.

- *"It doesn't sound like things are too bad, man. Why not just keep doing what you're doing?"*
- *"You said you'll get there eventually. Why not just double down?"*
- *"We don't want to fix what's not broken."*

This forces them to justify why they actually need help. They will paint themselves into a corner by explaining all the reasons their current approach isn't working.

## 4. Advanced Sales Psychology

### Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs in Sales

One of the core human needs is **stability.** If a prospect tells you "I'm stable right now," they just removed one of the needs you could leverage. You need to re-create the gap.

- Prospect: *"I'm stable, just not where I want to be."*
- You: *"Where do you want to be?"* → *"What do you need to get there?"* → *"What are you noticing that makes you feel you need that?"*

Re-gap by finding the distance between "stable" and "where I want to be."

### The Critical Factor — Bypassing Mental Defenses

There is a part of the brain that screens incoming information and decides whether to accept or reject it. Think of it as a guard standing at a castle gate.

**Direct challenges** ("You need to change") trigger the guard. The prospect's ego activates, and they deny, deflect, or shut down.

**Topic proximity** is the concept of how close your challenge is to the person. The further away from "you" the statement is, the easier it slips past the guard.

- ❌ Close proximity (triggers defense): *"You need to get better at this."*
- ✅ Far proximity (bypasses defense): *"I saw an article about how the most successful people in [field] are the ones who take risks. Would you agree?"*

**Techniques for bypassing the critical factor:**

1. **Pattern interrupts:** *"What if it wasn't what you thought it was?"* — creates momentary confusion, lowering the guard.
2. **Third-person framing:** *"What happens to the people who never try?"* — not "What happens to YOU," but "the people." Creates a subconscious agreement they can't deny.
3. **Stacking micro-agreements:** Like a lawyer in a courtroom — ask 10 small questions they agree with, then ask the big one. They've already primed their brain to say yes.

### The Subconscious Agreement Stack

This is the persuasion engine behind the whole methodology:

1. *"Have you considered that the people who never try something because they're scared are the same people that stay exactly where they're at?"* → Can't disagree.
2. *"Because what happens to the people that don't [take action]?"* → They stay stuck. Can't disagree.
3. *"So what's going to happen to you if you keep doing [X]?"* → Now they can't disagree because they already agreed to the abstract version.

You are getting agreement at a distance, then collapsing the distance back onto them.

### Once It's Fear, It's Always Fear

Critical principle: when a prospect reveals that their real objection is fear, **everything after that is also fear.** Do not get fooled by secondary objections that follow.

- Prospect reveals fear → then says *"I just need to go work on my mindset first"*
- This is NOT a separate objection. This is fear talking.
- Response: *"It makes sense you're saying that — because you're scared, which is fine. What's happening right now is your brain is finding all the reasons not to change because it's uncomfortable. My concern for you is that nothing changes without action."*

Do not let them hop off the call to "work on themselves" when the real issue is fear of commitment.

## 5. Alignment Phase

Alignment is where you surface the prospect's past patterns and limiting beliefs, then get them to see how those patterns have cost them.

### When the Prospect Is Sweeping Things Under the Rug

Watch for prospects who:
- Smile or laugh when talking about painful experiences
- Talk fast and breeze through difficult topics
- Immediately follow up pain with "but I'm getting better" or "it's not that bad"

**How to handle it — Call it out directly:**

*"Hey, can I make an observation? I don't say this to be malicious — I say it because I care and I want to bring awareness to it. I've noticed that every time you talk about how tough this has been, you've been smiling. Have you noticed that?"*

Then explain why: *"People sometimes try to make a joke out of something that's really painful. My concern is that if we don't look at the severity of this, it's going to cause you to feel like you don't need to change. And then we stay in the same spot. Is that what we want?"*

### Challenging Weak Commitment

When a prospect gives tentative language — "I'm pretty sure this is what I want," "I think I should" — challenge it immediately.

- Prospect: *"Yeah, I'm pretty sure this is what I want."*
- You: *"Rather than what you want — what do you need, though?"*
- Prospect: *"I need to make a change."*
- You: *"Why?"*

The distinction between "want" and "need" reappears throughout the entire call. Never let it slide.

### Testing Claims of Change

When a prospect says they're "working on overcoming it" or "getting better day by day":

- *"I'm working on overcoming it — by doing what?"*
- *"What is your definition of overcoming it? Because mine is investing in something."*
- *"And where has that gotten you up until now?"*

If they give you a vague answer: *"What do you consider change, then? Because that's not my definition of change. That's not the actual definition of change."*

### Challenging Contradictions

When a prospect's words contradict their actions:

- Prospect: *"I'm the type of person who always makes time."*
- But they've also told you they haven't been making time.
- You: *"Well, to be fair — up until now, you haven't been that guy. You mentioned you're working two jobs, you're sleeping in, you're not doing the things. So which one is it — are you making the time, or are you not?"*

Deliver this with care, not condescension. Preface with: *"I don't say this to be malicious..."*

## 6. Identity Selling

Identity selling is the deepest level of the close. It happens when the prospect gives you their full belief structure — who they are, how they make decisions, and why — based on their life experiences.

### The Identity = Experience + Belief + Decision-Making Pattern

- **Experience:** *"I got burned by a course before."*
- **Belief:** *"People just sell courses to make money, they don't actually care."*
- **Decision-making pattern:** *"That's why I never invest in help."*

This is their identity: *"I am someone who doesn't invest because I've been burned."*

### Identity Consequence vs. Outcome Consequence

Most closers consequence the outcome: *"What happens if you don't hit 20K/month?"*

Identity selling consequences the identity: *"What happens if you hold onto the identity of someone who never changes?"*

This is more powerful because:
- If they don't change their identity, they can't get to 20K/month anyway
- The outcome conversation becomes hypothetical without identity change
- It addresses the root cause, not the symptom

### When to Skip Desired State and Go Straight to Pitch

When you have the full identity-behavior gap (experience + belief + fear), you can take them through alignment and pitch immediately after. You don't need to explore desired state because you've already gone deeper than desired state — you're at identity level.

## 7. Objection Handling Framework (4 Phases)

### Phase 1: Identify the Real Objection (Diffuse & Clarify)

**The 5 objection categories:** Time, Money, Values, Spouse, Fear

**Diffuse first — don't react:**
- *"That's fine, and you could..."* (normalizing)

**Clarify — get the objection + the reason behind it:**
- *"What's the main thing you need to go over in your mind?"*
- *"That's not uncommon... is it a lot in comparison to your goals, or how do you mean?"*
- *"What's showing up for you that's causing you to say [X]?"*

**You need two things:** The objection itself AND why they're giving it to you. Examples:
- "It's a lot of money" + "because I've never spent that much before"
- "It's a lot of money" + "because I've looked at six quotes and they're all cheaper"
- "It's a lot of money" + "because I only have 6K in the bank"
- "It's a lot of money" + "because we've only been on this call 30 minutes"

Each of these requires a different reframe.

**If money, clarify deeper:**
- *"How do you mean you can't do it?"*
- *"What makes you say you don't have [X]?"*

**If partner/spouse:**
- *"How do you mean 'if it's the right move'?"*
- *"Where is that coming from?"*

### Phase 2: The "Aside" Test

Remove the objection temporarily to find the emotional driver:

- *"Money aside, would you do it?"*
- (They say yes)
- *"Why would YOU do it though?"* ← This is the hook/driver

You need an emotional hook you can return to throughout the reframe. This is their core motivation in their own words (scale the business, retire parents, leave their job, etc.).

### Phase 3: Reframe the Initial Objection

**Step 1 — Pattern interrupt:**
- *"What if it wasn't what you thought it was?"*

**Step 2 — Get permission to reframe:**
- *"Because I've been in your shoes... could I share a perspective?"*
- *"Would you be open to a different way of looking at this?"*

**Step 3 — Choose your reframe tool (use what fits, not all three):**

#### A. Perspective Reframe
Best when the objection is rooted in their decision-making pattern (*"this is just how I make decisions"*).

1. *"It makes sense you feel this way — we all make decisions based on our current perspective."*
2. *"Would you agree that our mindset and decisions dictate our outcomes?"*
3. *"Are you 100% satisfied with where you're at?"*
4. *"Do you want the way you've done things — that has gotten you here — to dictate your future?"*

#### B. Symptom vs. Problem Reframe
Best when the money objection is clearly a downstream effect of a business/life problem.

- *"Is not having the money the problem — or is it the symptom of the actual problem?"*
- *"What's the actual problem? Why don't you have the money? Why is it 'a lot'?"*

#### C. Risk Reframe
Best when you need to create a direct contrast between action and inaction.

- *"What's more risky: you staying where you're at and telling yourself 'I don't have the money'... or using a different perspective so you can overcome it and [get the hook/driver they gave you]?"*

### Phase 4: Final Reframe Loop (3 loops max, then logistics)

**Empathy opening:**
- *"You telling me [X] is normal because that is the comfortable decision..."*

**Consequence questions:**
- *"What happens if you keep telling yourself [X] and nothing changes? What does that look like?"*
- *"What if you never fix the real problem and keep doing [X] — what happens then?"*

**Flip it:**
- *"So with that in mind, why would I let you believe that [their objection] is the right thing for you?"*
- *"How is you telling yourself [X] helping you with [their goal]?"*

**Commitment questions:**
- *"Are you committed to being the new person who doesn't fall into these limitations?"*
- *"Why? Are you willing to let go of that? Because you don't have to."*
- *"So what do you need to do from here?"*

**Then move to logistics / pitch.**

**The 3-loop rule:** If you've used all three reframe tools (perspective, symptom vs. problem, risk) and they're still not budging — stop reframing. Move directly into logistics. *"Well, let's do this, my man..."* and walk them through the next steps.

## 8. Fear Objection — Special Handling

Fear is the most common hidden objection and requires its own playbook.

### Diffuse First
- *"What you're feeling is not uncommon. This is supposed to happen. It's a little bit of fear and nerves because you've never done this before — which is fine."*

### Metaphors & Analogies for Fear

**The Cliff Jump:**
- *"Have you ever jumped off a cliff into a lake? What's the hardest part? It's deciding to jump after you count down from 3. That's where you are right now. After you count down and choose NOT to jump — does it get easier or harder? The people who don't jump... how much regret do they have? So what's more expensive — a little fear, or a lifetime of regret?"*

**The Inception Scene:**
- *"You know the part in Inception where Leonardo DiCaprio is told: 'You don't know if I can get you back. But I can. So do you want to take a leap of faith — or die an old man filled with regret?' Which person do you want to be?"*

**The Car Accident:**
- *"Picture someone you care about who's been in two or three car accidents. They're hurt, but they're fine. Does that mean they should walk everywhere the rest of their life? So should your bad experiences stop you from ever trying anything again?"*

**The Overweight Person:**
- *"What happens to the overweight person who never steps into the gym because they're not sure they'll get results? They never know — because they never try."*

### Handling "How do I know it will work?"
- *"To be fair — you don't. And anyone promising you guaranteed results is probably doing something illegal. My question is: do you want those bad experiences to stop you from trying other things? How are you going to know if you never at least try?"*

### If They Have a Guarantee
Mention it, but don't let them buy because of it:
- *"We do have a guarantee. But if you're coming in just banking on a guarantee, maybe this isn't for you. My concern is that you've had those bad experiences, and [continue the reframe]."*

People who buy because of guarantees create bad clients and refund requests. You want them buying because of internal commitment, not external safety nets.

## 9. Behavioral Reads & Pattern Interrupts

### Detecting Behavior Changes (Baseline vs. Deviation)

Your job is to detect **change** from someone's baseline behavior. If someone is calm the whole call but starts speaking fast when you ask about responsibility — that's a deviation worth probing.

- If they externalize language ("you," "people," "they") only when discussing their responsibility → they're not owning it
- If they smile only when discussing painful topics → they're minimizing
- If they speed up only when describing failures → they're uncomfortable and rushing past it

### Don't Reciprocate Unhelpful Emotions

If the prospect laughs about something painful, **do not laugh with them.** You are validating their minimization pattern.

Instead, stay neutral or slightly serious. Let the silence do the work. Then redirect: *"I really want you to consider this because it's important."* Pause for 4 seconds. Let them sit with it.

### Slowing Down the Fast Talker

Some prospects rapid-fire through their problems to avoid feeling them. Slow them down:

- Pause after they finish.
- *"I really want you to sit with that for a second."*
- Lower your voice slightly.
- Ask a single, direct question and wait.

## 10. Question Bank — Career Coaching for Executives

### Process Audit Questions (Exposing Broken Systems)

- *"Walk me through what you've tried over the last 60–90 days to land a role."*
- *"How much time per week are you spending applying or networking right now?"*
- *"What should be happening by now that simply isn't?"*
- *"If someone at your level followed that same process for the next 6 months, what do you realistically expect would change?"* — forces admission that the system is broken
- *"How in control do you feel of this job search right now — on a scale from 1 to 10?"*
- *"Who is actually steering the outcome right now — you, or the market?"*
- *"How comfortable are you letting something this important stay that way?"*

### Identity-Based Questions (Executives Are Sensitive to Status Loss)

- *"How does this experience compare to how you've seen yourself operate at other points in your career?"*
- *"Is this process matching the level of seniority and impact you're used to?"*
- *"When you think about your body of work, does the market response surprise you?"*
- *"Does this feel like a reflection of your capability — or a misalignment in how you're being positioned?"*
- *"What's frustrating about that gap?"*
- *"How does it feel to be competing in the same pool as people far earlier in their careers?"*
- *"What's it like having to prove yourself again at this stage?"*
- *"Do you feel the market is recognizing your level — or lumping you in with everyone else?"*
- *"What does it cost you internally to accept less visibility or authority than you know you're capable of?"*

### Legacy & Investment Questions

- *"What have you spent the last 10–20 years becoming?"*
- *"How important is it that your next role builds on that — rather than resets it?"*
- *"What would feel unacceptable to compromise on at this point in your career?"*
- *"How do you want this chapter to read when you look back on it?"*

### Erosion Questions (Confidence Bleed)

- *"What has this process started to change about how you see yourself?"*
- *"What's the internal conversation you're having after another rejection or silence?"*
- *"How long can someone at your level tolerate that before it starts to bleed into confidence?"*
- *"What do you notice slipping — energy, certainty, conviction?"*

### Future Self Contrast

- *"Imagine yourself six months from now — what would success look like?"*
- *"Now imagine nothing changes — what worries you most about that version of you?"*
- *"Which version feels more aligned with who you know yourself to be?"*
- *"What would that future version thank you for deciding today?"*

### Accountability & Decision Questions

- *"Based on everything you've shared, does staying the course feel like leadership — or avoidance?"*
- *"What would someone you respect do in this position?"*
- *"If this were one of your direct reports, what would you advise them to change?"*
- *"At what point does investing in the right strategy become the most responsible move?"*

## 11. Pre-Call Priming & NLP Anchoring

Before every call, prime your mental state:

- *"I dictate the pace."*
- *"My energy is unshakable."*
- *"Nothing they say can affect me."*
- *"I am locked in."*

Reframe nervous energy: that's not nervousness — that's excitement. You're excited to put on a show and help someone.

## 12. Common Mistakes & How to Fix Them

| Mistake | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Skipping tangible goals at the top | Always get a specific, drawable outcome before going into pain |
| Going straight to pain without establishing need | Follow the sequence: Want → Current State → Need → Noticing |
| Asking about time frames too far in the past | Shrink the time gap — "the last 2-3 weeks" not "the last year" |
| Forcing a problem the prospect didn't mention | Use their words, not yours. If they said "annoying," probe on annoying — don't ask about confidence if they never mentioned it |
| Laughing when the prospect laughs about pain | Don't reciprocate minimization. Stay neutral and redirect |
| Accepting "I'm pretty sure" as commitment | Challenge it. "Pretty sure, or you need to? What do you need, though?" |
| Asking consequence questions about outcomes instead of identity | "What happens if you hold onto the identity of someone who never changes?" > "What happens if you don't hit 20K?" |
| Using all reframe tools on every objection | Pick the one that fits. If it doesn't land, try one more. After 3 loops, go to logistics |
| Pitching two different price points (options close) | Pitch one price. If it's too high, work down. Giving options triggers indecision on high-ticket |
| Not getting permission before reframing | Always ask: "Could I share a perspective?" / "Would you be open to a different way of looking at that?" |
| Letting a prospect leave the fear objection | Once it's fear, everything after is also fear. Stay on fear. Don't let them redirect to spouse, time, or "I need to think about it" |
| Using "support" in need questions | Say "what do you need specifically?" not "what do you need in terms of support?" — the word support softens the urgency |

## Quick Reference: The Full Call Flow

```
OPEN → Tangible Want → Current State → Specific Need → Noticing/Experience
  ↓
PROBLEM BUILDING → Probe feeling words → Shrink time gap → Create cortisol
  ↓
ALIGNMENT → Surface beliefs → Challenge contradictions → Call out minimization
  ↓
IDENTITY (if available) → Experience + Belief + Decision Pattern → Consequence identity
  ↓
PITCH → "What do you need in a [program/system]?" → Present solution
  ↓
OBJECTION HANDLING → Phase 1: Clarify → Phase 2: Aside Test → Phase 3: Reframe → Phase 4: Loop/Commit
  ↓
LOGISTICS → Payment → Onboarding
```

## Pricing Strategy Note

- Always pitch the higher-ticket option first.
- If the gap between tiers is too large (e.g., 20K → 5K), it diminishes the perceived value of the lower tier. Have a middle tier.
- Qualify by income/situation: prospects making over a certain threshold get pitched the premium tier with payment plan options. Below that threshold, pitch the standard tier.
- Never pitch multiple options simultaneously on high-ticket. That's an "options close" — it works for $7 protein bags, not $10,000 programs.
